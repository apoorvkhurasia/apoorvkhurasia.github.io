One of the most important things leaders do is make decisions. This should come as no surprise to anyone who has been in a leadership position but it often surprises people who are about to become a leader. You can be the best coach, the best mentor, the best technical expert, the best team advocate --- all things that may be in the job description for your new role and are no doubt important to a certain degree --- but if you do not enjoy making decisions, you will not be a successful leader.

What holds a leader back from making decisions is the uncertainty and the lack of confidence. None of us are experts at everything and the more senior we go in our positions, the fewer signals we get from the ground. How on earth can then we be expected to arrive at correct decisions all the time?

The answer is --- we are not.

We --- as leaders --- are expected to ensure a decision is made. And once it is made, we are accountable for it and must own it.

There is no unique recipe for it but what has worked for me and many successful leaders I have worked with is a framework. It does not need to be perfect. It does not have to answer everything that comes across your desk. The only thing it needs to do is ensure that it solves for the problems you are accountable for and adheres to the norms and values of your company.
